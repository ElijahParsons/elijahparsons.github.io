3D Printed 5x5 Rubik's Cube

The goal of this project is to 3D model and print a fully functional 5x5 Rubik's Cube. This requires precise modeling and tight tolerances to create interlocking parts that interact with several other parts simultaneously.

ROLE Designer & Manufacturer
DATE 2026
TOOLS Onshape, 3D printing

The Inspiration

I have always been interested in Rubik's Cubes and thought that it would be really cool to have a 3D printed cube. This led me to modeling and printing a 3x3 Rubik's Cube in a few days. The project difficulty did not satisfy me so I turned to the 5x5.

The goal for this project was to model and print a fully functional 5x5 Rubik's Cube. The only parts that I was allowing to not be 3D printed was 6 screws and springs to hold the core together

Engineering Goals

The requirements laid out for this project was for the arm to be standalone without the need of external help from a computer or cameras mounted somewhere other than on the robot. It also needs to be able to pick up a can of pop at its fully extended length of roughly 17 inches. It listens to commands from speech, interpretes them locally, and completes the given task.

  • Keeping all parts local to the robot arm
  • Moving a full can of pop autonomously
  • Respond to verbal commands
  • Locate parts through camera mounted on arm

Reverse Engineering the Cube

The first step in designing the cube was to disassemble a 5x5 that I had to see how the internals worked and interacted. The internals of a Rubik's Cube is complicated due to parts interlocking in a way that allows rotational movement that can pass off pieces to a new layer. This design restricts the pieces to be physically attached to the other pieces.

3D Modeling and Design

The 3D model was modeled in its entirety using Onshape. Each piece was designed as a rough model and added to an assembly to see how it interacts with each piece. This led to many iterations of each piece as each feature was fine tuned through several tweaks.

The most major modeling change was when the internals of the cube was designed using a square inside instead of a circle. This led to all five major pieces needing large redesigns. This was the only major change that was needed as every other change was continuous small adjustments. These modifications came from rigorous checking and the cube was thouroughly designed before any printing took place.

Printing the Cube

All parts except for the screws and springs were 3D printed: corner, outer edge, middle edge, center center, center edge, center corner, center cap, core. Screws and springs were purchased and the core was threaded for the screws.

The only part modification that happened after printing started was the shrinking of the core to allow more internal space for the parts to move around. Some non-critical part modifications were tested to allow for more high quality prints, however these changes did not affect the performance of the parts.

Final Result and Lessons Learned

When all the parts were printed the cube was assembled and stickers were applied. The cube fully functioned as intended and could complete the checkboard pattern which proves every layer has the ability to move.

The approach taken in designing this cube was to precisely and thouroughly model the parts before anything was printed and tested which turned out well because the main difficulty is the geometry of the parts rather than any structural component. This worked because there were no major iterations after the parts were printed, and it taught me that high quality up front work pays off and saves time in the long run.
